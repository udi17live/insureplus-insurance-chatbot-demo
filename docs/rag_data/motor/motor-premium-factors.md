# Motor Premium Factors

> The motor insurance premium is calculated from seven factors that reflect the risk profile of the driver and vehicle.

## Vehicle Age and Make

Older vehicles generally attract lower premiums for comprehensive cover because their market value is lower. High-performance or luxury makes carry higher premiums due to greater repair costs and theft risk. The vehicle manufacture year is used to estimate current market value.

## Driver Age

Younger drivers typically pay higher premiums due to statistically higher claim rates. Drivers under 25 attract the highest age-related loading. Premiums reduce progressively from age 25 onwards and stabilise around age 40.

## Annual Mileage

Higher annual mileage increases the probability of an accident and results in a higher premium. Insure Plus uses four mileage bands: under 5,000 miles, 5,000–10,000, 10,000–20,000, and over 20,000 miles per year.

## No-Claims Discount

Each year without a claim earns one NCD year. Insure Plus recognises up to 5 NCD years. Each NCD year reduces the base premium. Five NCD years provides the maximum discount. Making a claim resets NCD to zero unless protected NCD is purchased as an add-on.

## Coverage Type

Comprehensive cover carries a higher premium than third-party fire and theft, which is higher than third-party only. Choosing a lower coverage tier reduces the premium but also reduces the protection you receive.
